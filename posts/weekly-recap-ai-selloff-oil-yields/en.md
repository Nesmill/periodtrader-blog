Last week (October 19–23, 2026) markets were pulled between two worries: that the AI spending boom is running into a financing wall, and that the war with Iran could flare up again. Tech stocks had their worst day since June, the 10-year Treasury yield had its biggest one-day drop since March 2023, and Brent crude rose for a third straight week.

Below: what changed, why it matters if you trade systematically, and what is still unresolved before a week packed with central bank decisions and big tech earnings.

## 1. AI stocks: the worst Nasdaq day since June

On Thursday, October 22, the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.6% to 26,008.86, its worst session since June, and the S&P 500 lost 1.21%, its worst day since July. The Dow finished slightly higher. Meta dropped about 7% even though its revenue beat expectations, after it raised its capital spending forecast. Nvidia fell about 4%, AMD more than 8% and the AI cloud provider CoreWeave 10%. Reuters reported that the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 6.7%, its biggest daily drop since June.

The worry is less about demand than about money. Nvidia's CEO Jensen Huang told CNBC that demand for specialised AI cloud providers is "off the charts", but that capital is scarce. An analyst quoted by CBS News noted that AI spending is outpacing operating cash flow, which leaves companies reliant on debt and equity markets.

The selloff was narrow. Over the full week the Nasdaq lost 0.7%, while the Dow gained 0.4% and the small-cap Russell 2000 gained 2%. On Friday the pattern flipped: chip stocks bounced, and the Dow fell 1.2% as selling spread to other sectors.

### Why systematic traders care

A strategy trading a Nasdaq index and the same strategy on the Dow had very different weeks, even though both are "US stocks". And on Thursday, AI-related stocks fell together, which is how a basket of five AI names can end up behaving like one large position.

## 2. Treasuries: the 10-year dips below 5%, then bounces back

The US Treasury's daily data show the 10-year yield at 5.13% on Wednesday and 4.96% on Thursday, a drop of 17 basis points in one session. CNBC and Reuters both called it the biggest one-day decline since March 10, 2023, and it took the yield below 5% for the first time since September 23. By Friday the 10-year was back at 5.00%, down 9 basis points for the week.

The interesting part is the direction. Earlier this month, oil and inflation worries had pushed 10- and 30-year yields to their highest levels in more than two decades. On Thursday, investors bought Treasuries as stocks fell. "For the first time in a while, Treasurys are enjoying haven status," one fixed-income manager told CNBC.

### Why systematic traders care

The relationship between stocks and bonds is not fixed. If your portfolio or your hedge assumes bonds rise when stocks fall, check how that relationship behaved over the period you backtested. This month showed it can flip within weeks.

## 3. Oil: Brent up 6% on renewed Iran strike fears

Brent crude futures rose 4% on Thursday to $113.17 and another 3.1% on Friday to $116.72, the highest settlement since April 29, according to CNBC. Brent gained 6.2% over the week, its third weekly gain in a row. US crude (WTI) settled at $103.27 on Friday, up about 2% for the week.

The trigger was a New York Times report that the US and Israel have plans for new strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections, plus President Trump's comment on Wednesday that the US was not negotiating with Tehran and that something would happen "fairly soon". Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia continued during the week. As of Monday morning, no new strikes had been reported; on Sunday, Trump told Fox News it was "getting to be decision time" between a deal and resuming attacks, while Qatari mediators visited Tehran. The last confirmed US strikes on Iran were on September 1, according to CNBC.

### A roll trap on your Brent chart

December Brent futures stop trading on Friday, October 30. Under ICE's rules, a Brent contract expires on the last business day of the second month before delivery. On Friday, December settled at $116.72 while January, the next contract, closed at $111.70 on Yahoo Finance's data: a gap of $5.02, or 4.3%.

On Monday morning (about 9:05 a.m. ET), December Brent was trading near $115.30, about 1.2% below Friday's settlement. But a continuous chart that had already switched to January showed about $110.30, which looks like a 5.5% drop from $116.72. Most of that "drop" is the change of contract, not the market.

If you backtest or trade on continuous futures or on Brent CFDs, check how your data and your broker handle the roll. An unadjusted roll gap can trigger stops and signals that never happened in a tradable contract.

## 4. Crypto: ETF flows whipsaw while bitcoin slips toward $80,000

US spot bitcoin ETFs had a whiplash week. Early inflows reversed on Thursday with about $635 million of net outflows, the largest single day since June 25 according to SoSoValue data cited by Cointelegraph. Decrypt's tracker put it at $635.6 million, which erased nearly 60% of the roughly $1.07 billion the funds had taken in over the previous three sessions.

Bitcoin itself moved less than the flows suggest. It closed Friday at $81,074, down 0.5% for the week, after touching about $84,800 on Wednesday. Over the weekend it slipped to a Sunday close of $80,024, about 5.7% below Wednesday's high.

### Why systematic traders care

Crypto trades through the weekend while stock, bond and futures markets are closed, so with an Iran decision pending, bitcoin was one of the few markets that could react in real time. Weekend liquidity is thinner, though: Yahoo Finance's data show Sunday's reported bitcoin volume at about half of Friday's. It is worth checking a crypto backtest on weekends and weekdays separately.

## 5. Forex: the yen firms as US yields fall

USD/JPY opened the week at 157.79 and reached 158.68 on October 20, according to MUFG Research. As risk appetite faded on Thursday, the pair broke below 157, this month's low, and fell under 156.50 early on Friday before recovering toward 157. On Yahoo Finance's daily data it lost about 1.0% for the week. EUR/USD ended practically unchanged around 1.12.

### Why systematic traders care

USD/JPY often follows the gap between US and Japanese interest rates, so a large move in Treasuries can show up in the yen within hours. With the Fed and the Bank of Japan both deciding this week, a yen strategy faces two rate decisions within about two days.

## The week ahead: what is still uncertain

- **Fed, Wednesday October 28 (2:00 p.m. ET).** The target range has been 3.75% to 4.00% since the September hike. CME FedWatch pricing cited by Trading Economics showed about a 9% chance of a hike this week and more than 80% for December. Pricing can change quickly.
- **Microsoft and Alphabet (Wednesday after the close) and Amazon (Thursday after the close).** Their spending plans sit at the center of the AI financing debate described above.
- **ECB on Thursday, October 29, and the Bank of Japan on October 29–30.**
- **Iran.** Whether talks continue or strikes resume is still open, with US midterm elections on November 3.

Spreads often widen and prices can gap around decisions like these.
