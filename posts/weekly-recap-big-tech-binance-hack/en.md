Last week (October 26–30, 2026) the AI spending debate moved from forecasts to cash. Alphabet had its worst day on record, Intel its worst since 1974, and the S&P 500 its worst session since June. Three central banks held rates, and crypto absorbed what analysts call the largest theft in history: $6.3 billion taken from Binance.

Below: what changed, why it matters for systematic traders, and what is still open.

## 1. Big tech earnings: AI spending meets the cash-flow test

Investors read free cash flow, the money left after capital spending, more closely than revenue.

- **Alphabet** reported negative free cash flow of $9.07 billion for the third quarter, against roughly $2.7 billion expected, and kept its 2026 capital spending guidance at $195–205 billion. Search revenue also missed. On Thursday the stock fell nearly 13%, its worst day since Google went public in 2004, erasing about $500 billion of market value (CNBC).
- **Amazon** spent $77.3 billion on capital expenditures in the quarter, up from $35.1 billion a year earlier and well above the roughly $58.7 billion analysts expected. Free cash flow over the last twelve months was minus $42.4 billion. The shares closed Friday down 1.4%.
- **Intel** fell 28.6% on Friday, its worst day since 1974, even though its quarterly profit beat expectations. Yahoo Finance reported fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $15–16 billion against about $17 billion expected, and earnings per share of $0.25 against $0.43, as rising memory prices weigh on PC demand.

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 1.8% and the Nasdaq 2.9%. The Nasdaq Composite lost 5.2% in October, Yahoo Finance reported.

### Why systematic traders care

A stop-loss is an order, not a guarantee. Intel's drop happened between Thursday's close and Friday's open, so a stop 5% below Thursday's price would have filled near the open, far below its level. If a strategy holds single stocks through earnings, the backtest should assume gap fills and size for a move several times the stop distance. Our post on [trading costs and slippage](/blog/trading-costs-spread-commission-slippage) shows how quickly that eats into expectancy.

## 2. Central banks: three holds, a new Fed calendar and an early ECB exit

### Federal Reserve

On Wednesday the Fed kept its target range at 3.75% to 4.00% in a 10–2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike. Chair Kevin Warsh also announced that from 2027 one of the eight annual meetings will focus only on strategy, with no rate vote expected (two such meetings in 2028).

Thursday's PCE inflation reading was 3.6% year over year, CNBC reported, yet Treasuries rallied as stocks fell. In the Treasury's daily data the 10-year yield went from 5.05% on Wednesday to 4.94% on Friday, and the 2-year yield fell 13 basis points over the week.

### ECB

On Thursday the European Central Bank left its deposit rate at 2.50% and warned that higher energy prices pose upside risks to inflation. Hours earlier, President Christine Lagarde announced she will step down on February 7, 2027, about nine months before her term ends. She said she wants "the freedom to engage on matters that are not monetary policy matters"; one economist told AP the exit "does not change the outlook for ECB policies".

### Bank of Japan

On Friday the BOJ held its policy rate at 1.25%, the highest since 1995, in an 8–1 vote. For the first time since the target was set in 2013, it said underlying inflation is "generally consistent" with 2%. The yen did not strengthen: USD/JPY rose 0.8% over the week to 157.80, and EUR/USD slipped 0.3%.

### Why systematic traders care

Many strategies pause around central bank decisions. Those filters depend on a calendar, and from 2027 the Fed will have fewer rate-setting days, so a hard-coded list of FOMC dates will need updating. The yen also shows that what one economist called a "hawkish hold" does not reliably move a currency in the textbook direction.

## 3. Crypto: a record $6.3 billion hack at Binance

Late on Thursday, October 29, attackers moved about $6.3 billion out of Binance wallets, according to the exchange and blockchain analysts. Most of it happened in about 20 minutes, and several analytics firms attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-linked unit; Binance said its own investigation had not attributed responsibility. The previous record was the $1.5 billion stolen from Bybit in February 2025.

Binance suspended withdrawals, said about $3 billion had been frozen, and set aside more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to cover customer losses. Phased withdrawals resumed on Sunday, November 1, Euronews reported.

US spot bitcoin ETFs had $643 million of net outflows on Friday, their first trading day after the hack, the largest since June 25 according to SoSoValue and Farside data. Bitcoin fell from $81,268 on Wednesday to $77,198 at Friday's close, down 4.8% for the week, and recovered to $78,248 on Sunday.

### Why systematic traders care

Exchange risk is separate from market risk: during the pause, nobody could move funds off Binance. Two habits help on any exchange: give bots API keys with trading permission only (no withdrawals), restricted to known IP addresses where the exchange allows it, and avoid keeping more on any one venue than a strategy needs.

## 4. Oil: Brent's December contract expires at a six-month high

Brent's December futures settled at $117.64 on Friday, their last trading day, up 2.4% on the day and 0.8% on the week, after briefly touching $118. The National reported a six-month high and a monthly gain of about 15%. Two tankers were struck in the Strait of Hormuz within 24 hours, one by explosives attached to its hull, according to UK Maritime Trade Operations.

### The roll, one week later

Last week we flagged that December Brent would expire on October 30. At expiry, December settled at $117.64 and January at $113.43: a gap of $4.21, or 3.6%. On Monday morning (7:30 a.m. ET), January Brent was at $108.96, down 3.9%, after reports that Washington and Tehran are both seeking diplomacy, CNBC reported. A continuous chart that joins December's last settlement to January's price shows a 7.4% drop; roughly half of it is the contract change.

## The week ahead: what is still uncertain

- **US midterm elections, Tuesday November 3.** CNBC, citing The Wall Street Journal and The New York Times, reported that the US is preparing for possible military action against Iran after the vote, while talks mediated by Qatar continue.
- **Jobs data.** JOLTS on Tuesday, ADP on Wednesday and the October payrolls report on Friday, November 6. LSEG data cited by Dow Jones put the odds of a Fed hike on December 9 at about 76%.
- **Bank of England, Thursday November 5**, expected to raise rates.
- **Apple earnings**, Monday after the close.

Spreads often widen and prices can gap around events like these.
